# Business case for model-based share portfolios vs adviser stock picking, and dispersion metrics/limits

Method note: web search returned summarised results; fetching of professionaladviser.com and morningstar.com failed (host not in session's allowed network domains), so figures from those sites are from search summaries and not verified against the full page. FANews page was fetched in full. Treat items flagged "unverified at source" accordingly.

## 1. Industry shift to CIPs / model portfolios (UK, US, SA): reasons, adoption, outcome evidence

### Takeaway
Centralisation is now the norm in UK advice (c.86-88% of firms use a CIP) and US model assets are growing 40%+ a year, driven mainly by time, consistency and scalability. Evidence that models improve client *returns* is thin; the best-supported benefits are time saved, lower outcome variability and process discipline.

### Cited Findings
- UK: Lang Cat survey (June 2025, 235 firms) found about 86% of adviser firms use a CIP (per search summary; page not fetched) — [Professional Adviser](https://www.professionaladviser.com/news/3070657/central-investment-proposition-lang-cat-survey)
- Earlier Lang Cat figures cited: 88% (State of the Adviser Nation, Q4 2021) and 82% in an older piece — same source and search summary above. These are inconsistent over time/definition; the 86-88% range is the safe statement.
- Lang Cat "Centralised Investment Propositions 2026" reportedly finds 63% of firms outsource versus 23% mostly managing portfolios themselves (secondary citation in search summary; not verified).
- UK retirement-proposition analogue: NextWealth/Brooks Macdonald survey found 56% of advisers have a formal centralised retirement proposition; 34% have no plans to introduce one — [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/new-nextwealth-guide-released-today-shows-how-the-centralised-retirement-proposition-crp-for-advisers-has-become-a-consistent-retirement-playbook/). Note this is CRP not CIP; I found no NextWealth CIP-adoption figure.
- UK MPS assets reported up 32% to GBP190bn as adviser usage grows (headline only; date not confirmed) — [International Adviser](https://international-adviser.com/mps-assets-surge-32-to-190bn-as-adviser-usage-grows)
- US: Morningstar reports third-party model portfolio assets of about USD934bn at end March 2026, +46% year on year (a second passage gives >USD943bn); BlackRock largest at USD308bn; custom models USD258bn, +40% — [Morningstar 2026 US Model Portfolio Landscape](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/blog/2026-us-model-portfolio-landscape)
- US: Cerulli (2024 report) says share of US adviser assets at firms identifying as model users reached 13% at end 2023; projected asset-allocation model assets USD2.9trn by 2026 — [Cerulli](https://www.cerulli.com/reports/us-asset-allocation-model-portfolios-2024)
- US: Broadridge says the model portfolio industry reached USD9.3trn at end 2025 and projects USD18.6trn by 2030 (broader definition than Morningstar/Cerulli; not comparable) — [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investing-strategies/model-portfolios-projected-to-reach-186t-by-2030)
- Time: Cerulli finds advisers using outside-built models spend about 10.6% of hours on investment management versus roughly 25% for those building their own — [Financial Planning](https://www.financial-planning.com/news/model-portfolios-are-just-one-way-advisors-save-time) (as summarised in search; verify)
- Advisor Perspectives commentary: investment management is about 15% of adviser time (300-400 hours a year); its data show model-based accounts had roughly half the return dispersion of a building-block (adviser-selected funds) approach — [Advisor Perspectives, Nov 2019](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2019/11/25/making-the-most-of-model-portfolios). Older than 2020 and vendor-adjacent; this is the only direct dispersion-vs-model-use datapoint found.
- Vanguard Adviser's Alpha: headline up to about 3% net return from adviser value, mostly behavioural coaching (up to ~1.5%), portfolio construction/low cost (~1%+), rebalancing up to ~0.35%; modelled and illustrative, not annual guaranteed, and not a measure of models vs adviser stock picking — [PlanAdviser](https://www.planadviser.com/vanguard-touts-five-ways-to-boost-returns/); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/advisers-can-add-3-percentage-points-to-clients-net-returns-56824)
- SA: Morningstar SA article (20 Mar 2024, by a DFM provider employee, promotional) lists reasons for outsourcing: portfolio management takes ~15% of adviser time (300+ hours a year); coaching demand; specialisation; cost of research/DD; rebalancing discipline (SA advisers generally need client permission for each change on non-discretionary books); offshore/fund complexity — [FANews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/intermediaries-brokers/7/general/1227/why-outsourcing-investment-management-adoption-is-growing/39057)
- Kitces: no Kitces Research content found in searches.

### Inferences
- Strongest defensible arguments are operational (time, consistency, oversight, scalability), not alpha. A business case should not promise higher returns; it should promise lower outcome variance and demonstrable process.
- Adviser's Alpha supports "value of discipline and rebalancing", but should not be cited as evidence for central stock selection.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or regulator evidence that centrally run models deliver better net client outcomes than adviser-picked portfolios.
- No Citywire/FT Adviser/Money Marketing piece on CIP drivers retrieved in full; Lang Cat reasons for CIP adoption not captured.
- No NextWealth CIP adoption number; no Kitces data.

## 2. Reputational, conduct and operational risks of inconsistent client outcomes

### Takeaway
I found no FCA finding specifically penalising firms whose clients in the same proposition got different investment outcomes. The regulatory hooks are the Consumer Duty, DISP complaint handling and the FCA's planned MPS review; key-person risk is argued by industry commentary rather than quantified.

### Cited Findings
- FCA ongoing-advice review (published 24 Feb 2025): 22 large firms, 7 years of data; suitability reviews delivered in ~83% of promised cases, 15% clients declined/no response, <2% no attempt; redress likely where no attempt; FCA says not systemic but will monitor complaints; sample not representative — [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/02/fca-publishes-findings-from-review-of-ongoing-financial-advice-services/)
- FCA to start a multi-firm review of model portfolio services applying Consumer Duty, with good/bad practice guidance; commentators note two suitability layers (adviser on client; MPS provider on adviser) — [Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102khcq/are-the-storm-clouds-gathering-for-mps-providers/)
- FCA 2026 consumer investments priorities: significant consolidation and rapid growth among platforms and MPS providers; MPS review with findings expected by Q1 2027 (law-firm summary; verify against FCA text) — [Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102mm1y/fca-sets-out-its-2026-regulatory-priorities-for-consumer-investments)
- FCA retirement income review assessed 24 firms' centralised retirement propositions; some had not adapted advice model/investment range for retirees (via search summary, law-firm sources) — [Regulation Tomorrow, Mar 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/03/fca-regulatory-priorities-report-2026-consumer-investments/)
- Key-person risk: Morningstar defines it as reliance on a person for decision making where exit/distraction hurts performance; centralised, person-independent process is the common mitigant (fund-manager context, not adviser stock picking) — [Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/funds/are-star-fund-managers-a-blessing-or-a-curse); [Citywire](https://citywire.com/selector/news/succession-success-eight-ways-ams-can-replace-managers/a1504045)

### Inferences
- Where a firm markets "a house proposition", materially different account returns across clients with the same mandate are hard to defend under Consumer Duty outcome monitoring and are a plausible complaint trigger; this is an inference, not a documented FCA finding.
- Adviser-picked portfolios create key-person and concentration risks (departure of adviser, unreviewed holdings); SA FSCA equivalents (TCF/FAIS, PPR) not researched.

### Gaps
- No FCA/FOS case or thematic finding on same-proposition dispersion; no complaints statistics linked to dispersion.
- No source quantifying key-person risk in adviser stock picking.

## 3. Practical dispersion metrics and controls; typical thresholds

### Takeaway
GIPS gives the formal definition of internal dispersion; operational limits (tolerance bands, % accounts within X% of model) are firm-set and I found no public authoritative "typical" thresholds for share portfolios.

### Cited Findings
- GIPS internal dispersion: measured only on portfolios in the composite for the full year; firm chooses and must disclose the measure (high/low, range, or standard deviation, equal- or asset-weighted); not required if five or fewer portfolios in composite — [GIPS Q&A](https://www.gipsstandards.org/?GIPSID=376); [Spaulding Group](https://spauldinggrp.com/dispersion-around-exactly-question-worth-pondering/)
- Asset-weighted dispersion can be dominated by a single large account; fee-schedule differences inflate net-of-fee dispersion — [Spaulding Group](https://spauldinggrp.com/gips-dispersion-returns-gross-net-fee/)
- Tolerance bands: Dimensional's study example uses 1-3% bands (e.g. 2% band on 60/40 triggers at 58/62); tighter bands reduce tracking deviation but raise turnover (1% band: 7.1% annualised one-way turnover if immediate vs 4.7% if spread over 20 trading days) — [Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/gb-en/insights/portfolio-rebalancing-all-at-once-or-little-by-little). Asset-class bands, not single-stock; a proxy only.
- Overlays/corridors as cost-efficient rebalancing tools — [CFA L3 study notes](https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/cfa-level3-rebalancing-and-overlays-transaction-cost-models-and-corridors)

### Inferences (suggested framework, not sourced thresholds)
- Candidate KPIs: (a) range and standard deviation of 12-month account returns vs model, equal- and asset-weighted, net and gross of fee; (b) % of accounts within +/-X% of model (X to be set by the IC, e.g. start from observed baseline); (c) per-account tracking error vs model; (d) holdings overlap/active share vs model (sum of min weights); (e) stock-level weight band breaches and days out of band; (f) exceptions list with reason codes (client restrictions, tax/CGT, cash, minimum trade size, late funding); (g) trade-allocation timing gap (rotation fairness).
- Set limits relative to a measured baseline of current adviser-picked dispersion, then tighten; explain unavoidable dispersion (fees, cash flows, restrictions) separately from avoidable (adviser overrides, execution lag).
- Test of the business case: compute current cross-account dispersion for one mandate over 12 months and compare to dispersion in a model-run pilot.

### Gaps
- No public source quoting specific thresholds (e.g. "90% of accounts within 1%/2% of model"); no sourced overlap/active-share limits between accounts.

## 4. Reporting dispersion to investment committees and clients

### Takeaway
Not found in public sources; only GIPS composite disclosure is documented.

### Cited Findings
- GIPS requires disclosure of the dispersion measure used and presentation per composite per year — [GIPS Q&A](https://www.gipsstandards.org/?GIPSID=376)

### Inferences
- A one-page IC pack could show: model vs median account return, dispersion range, % in tolerance, top five exceptions, drift heatmap, trades pending. Illustrative design, unsourced.

### Gaps
- No public dashboard/KPI examples from DFMs or wealth managers retrieved.

## 5. South African specifics

### Takeaway
SA DFM assets grew strongly 2019-2021 and DFM use is driven by adviser time and rebalancing permissions; current (2024-26) DFM market size and named firms' share model practices are poorly documented in what I could retrieve.

### Cited Findings
- First Collaborative Exchange DFM survey: surveyed DFMs managed close to R200bn, estimated ~10% of R2.4trn in unit trusts/CIS — [Cover](https://www.cover.co.za/news/new-report-shines-light-into-the-future-of-south-africas-dfm-sector) (undated in summary; c.2020-21)
- Larger DFMs grew rand assets >15% CAGR over three years to 31 Dec 2021; Collaborative Exchange expected regulation and saturation to slow growth — same source family (Bravura): [Bravura](https://www.bravurasolutions.com/?p=22984)
- Other estimates conflict (e.g. >40 DFMs/R165bn vs >100 DFMs/about 20% of retail AUM; both undated) — [Dispatch Live, 29 May 2020](https://www.dispatchlive.co.za/business/2020-05-29-what-do-you-know-about-who-is-choosing-your-funds/)
- SA CIS industry AUM R4.58trn at end 2025 (denominator context) — [Daily Investor](https://dailyinvestor.com/investing/128262/south-africas-largest-asset-managers-revealed/) (as per search summary)
- Sanlam Private Wealth offers equity, multi-asset and income portfolios by risk category and a wrap for advisers outsourcing to DFMs — [SPW](https://sanlamprivatewealth.sanlam.com/what-we-offer/investment-management/discretionary-investment-management); [SPW Apr 2026 product page](https://sanlamprivatewealth.sanlam.com/media/documents/1573_SPW_Product_Page_SOUTH_AFRICAN_INVESTMENT_PORTFOLIOS_Apr26_FA_HR_NC.pdf)

- Anchor Capital managed share portfolios mentioned via Crowe partnership — [Crowe](https://www.crowe.com/za/services/wealth) (thin)
- SA DFM structures: own unit trust FoF, model on platform, or bespoke; FSCA considering reporting standards for FoF and model portfolios — Bravura link above
- Sanlam multi-manager CEO op-ed (Business Day, 14 Sep 2026) says advisers increasingly delegate to multimanagers/DFMs; promotional — [Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/opinion/2026-09-14-darryl-moodley-how-multimanagers-are-redefining-returns/)

### Inferences
- Non-discretionary SA books (client consent needed per trade) are a structural cause of dispersion; a discretionary mandate or pre-authorised model switching is the main lever (supported by the Morningstar SA point on consent).
